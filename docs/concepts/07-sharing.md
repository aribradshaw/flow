# Sharing

The owner of an environment can share part of it with another Flow user, by email: a person and everything below them, hand-picked cards, a project, a DevOps label, or the whole environment. The Share dialog (a card's **Share…** button, or Settings › Environment › *Share this environment…*) shows a live preview of exactly what the other person will see. Permissions are **view**, **comment** or **edit**.

The recipient finds it under Settings › Sharing › *Shared with you* and adds it to one of their own environments. The shared cards appear there as linked boxes with their own layout. Direct co-owners of a shared card show as read-only name stubs, and nothing else of theirs is shared. Edits travel both ways as the permission allows. Shared timeline items and DevOps cards carry the same link badge and name their owner, and fields the permission does not allow are locked. A shared card can be assigned to its share's members: the owner and the people it is shared with. If two people change the same field at once, the last change wins, and the person whose change was replaced sees "Changed by … just now" on that field, with the previous value and a one-click restore. Revoking a share removes everything from the recipient's environment. Recipients cannot share further; instead they ask the owner, who approves or denies the request.

A whole environment shared with you can also be **opened** as itself: it is listed in the account menu and Settings › Environment as "<environment> · shared by <owner>" with your permission, and opening it shows the owner's charts, timeline, DevOps board and AgentFlow as they have them, so you work in the same place. What you may change is what the share allows. AgentFlow agents and workflows come along too; an edit share can change their design and delegate cards, while an agent's Live switch, runner, models, schedule, instructions and tools stay with the owner, because they decide what runs on the owner's machines. Its 3D office is the owner's office, so everyone it is shared with meets there. When the share is revoked, the app says so and opens your own workspace.

The server enforces all of this: linked copies are computed from the owner's data on every read and are never stored in the recipient's environment. API keys and MCP see the shares placed in your own environments the same way, with the same permissions. The owner token used by the CLI sees each environment's own stored data only. Details: `docs/sharing.md`.

Anyone with a OneProfileID account can join Flow, and members can invite friends to the app itself (Settings › Account › *Invite to Flow*). A new person gets their own empty environment and sees nothing of anyone else's until it is shared with them.
